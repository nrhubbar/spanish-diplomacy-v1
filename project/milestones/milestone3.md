# Milestone 3: Recruitment, Reinforcements, And Income

## 1. Goal

Milestone 3 adds a small, deterministic wartime economy to the completed Milestone 2 scenario. Players earn income from controlled objectives, secretly spend it on recruitment and reinforcement orders, and receive new or restored formations through the same simultaneous submission and resolution pipeline.

This milestone must preserve the front-door order rule: local, computer, and future online actors use the same planning view, order gateway, validation, locking, reveal, and resolution services.

## 2. Scope

Milestone 3 includes:

- Faction resource balances.
- Income generated by controlled territories and cities.
- Scenario-defined recruitment locations and unit availability.
- Recruitment orders for new formations.
- Reinforcement orders for depleted formations.
- Secret spending until reveal.
- Deterministic validation and resolution ordering.
- Computer-player recruitment and reinforcement decisions.
- Income and spending summaries.

Milestone 3 does not include trade, loans, lend-lease, foreign intervention events, production queues longer than one turn, or a detailed national economy.

## 3. Historical And Scenario Data

Research and cite the industrial, population, port, depot, and mobilization significance used to assign income or recruitment capability. Treat income values as balanced abstractions informed by history, not literal pesetas or production totals.

Each scenario objective may define:

```ts
interface EconomicObjective {
  readonly territoryId: TerritoryId;
  readonly cityId?: CityId;
  readonly income: number;
  readonly recruitmentTags: readonly RecruitmentTag[];
  readonly sourceIds: readonly string[];
  readonly designNote: string;
}
```

Every value must have both a historical rationale and a balance note. Changing an economic value must not require editing engine code.

## 4. Economy Rules

- Income is awarded during cleanup after movement, combat, control, and victory evaluation.
- Only uncontested controlled objectives produce income.
- A city inside a contested territory produces no income unless the controller also satisfies the scenario's supply rule.
- Resource balances are public after cleanup.
- Planned spending and purchase choices remain secret until reveal.
- A faction cannot spend more than its start-of-turn available balance.
- Failed orders refund their cost unless the scenario explicitly defines a consumed mobilization cost.
- No faction may have a negative resource balance.

The exact income scale and starting balances are scenario tuning values established during implementation playtests.

## 5. Recruitment

- Recruitment creates a new unit with a stable ID and scenario-defined formation type.
- A recruit order names the recruitment location and requested unit type.
- The location must be controlled, uncontested, supplied, and tagged to recruit that type.
- Multiple recruit orders competing for a location's capacity resolve deterministically according to scenario rules declared before submission.
- Newly recruited units cannot move during the turn in which they are created.
- Recruitment limits and costs live in scenario data.

## 6. Reinforcements

- Units gain a small strength/readiness model only to the extent required for reinforcement.
- A reinforce order names an existing friendly unit and spends resources to restore it toward its scenario-defined maximum.
- The target must exist, remain friendly, and satisfy location/supply requirements at resolution time.
- Reinforcement cannot exceed the unit's maximum strength.
- Destroyed units cannot be reinforced unless a separate replacement rule is explicitly added later.

## 7. Simultaneous Resolution Order

Use an explicit deterministic sequence:

1. Reveal all movement, recruitment, and reinforcement orders.
2. Revalidate spending against start-of-turn balances.
3. Resolve reinforcement timing as defined by the combat model.
4. Resolve movement and conflict.
5. Resolve recruitment at locations still eligible after conflict.
6. Apply refunds and final spending.
7. Evaluate victory.
8. Award end-of-turn income if the game is not over.
9. Emit a structured economy summary.

The implementation plan must settle whether reinforcement affects same-turn combat before code is written. The chosen timing must be visible in previews and resolution explanations.

## 8. Computer Player Behavior

Computer actors receive only their own balance, their legal economic orders, and public board state. They do not receive opponents' hidden purchases.

Initial scoring priorities:

- Reinforce a threatened formation near a historically motivated objective.
- Recruit at a supplied location near an active front.
- Preserve a small reserve when no useful legal purchase exists.
- Never spend resources merely to exhaust the budget.

Computer economic orders pass through the same front-door gateway and validation used by local players.

## 9. UI Work

- Show current public resources and projected cost in the order builder.
- Add recruit and reinforce actions alongside movement.
- Show eligible locations, unit types, costs, and capacity.
- Explain why an economic order is illegal.
- Keep submitted purchases hidden during other players' planning.
- Reveal spending with all other orders.
- Show recruits, reinforcements, refunds, income, and ending balances in resolution.
- Include economic changes in turn history.

## 10. Acceptance Criteria

- Controlled eligible objectives generate scenario-defined income.
- Contested or ineligible objectives do not generate income.
- Players can submit valid recruitment and reinforcement orders.
- Overspending and invalid locations are rejected by the engine.
- Purchases remain hidden until reveal.
- Failed refundable orders return their cost deterministically.
- New units receive stable IDs and cannot move on their recruitment turn.
- Reinforcement cannot exceed maximum unit strength.
- Computer players make legal, motivation-aware economic choices without hidden opponent data.
- Local and computer economic orders use the same gateway.
- Resolution explains every balance change.
- Victory remains terminal and does not award unnecessary post-game income.
- Build, unit tests, coverage thresholds, and Playwright flows pass.

## 11. Test Plan

- Income from controlled, contested, captured, and lost objectives.
- Legal and illegal recruitment locations and unit types.
- Recruitment capacity conflicts.
- Legal reinforcement, over-reinforcement, destroyed targets, and targets displaced by combat.
- Overspending across multiple hidden orders.
- Refund and non-refund cases.
- Stable recruited-unit identifiers and replay determinism.
- Privacy equivalence when opponent purchases change.
- Computer priorities with deterministic scoring and tie-breaking.
- Full all-local and mixed-actor turns with movement and economic orders.
- Resolution and history presentation on desktop and mobile.

## 12. Deferred

- Player-to-player trade and resource transfers.
- Foreign aid events and embargoes.
- Multi-turn production queues.
- Research or technology trees.
- Detailed supply networks and logistics throughput.
- Inflation, debt, civilian production, and national morale.
