# North debrief

1. Winning and plan change. The briefing said win by 10 of 18 territories or 19 of 25 cities, and take Madrid. On turn 1 I treated it as "hold what I have, relieve Oviedo, recruit the best unit I can afford". By turn 3 I saw the Southern command was conquering faster (Andalucia, Murcia, a 42-48 then 54-54 stack) and my funds were tiny, so I became passive and waited. On turn 4, once funds income jumped to about +29, I switched to economy plus a deathball (veterans, Mola, tanks) to take Cantabria and later Cataluna or Madrid. I never reached a real offensive in six turns. Turn 1 redo: spend the 15 Funds on Factory (4) and Mines (3) at Navarra plus one cheap unit instead of a veteran and an infantry, because income grew from about 6 to about 30 mostly by turn 4 (I am not certain how much was buildings versus taking Pais Vasco; I only saw the Funds jump and the builds finish at about the same time). I would also not leave Asturias on the default attack for several turns, which exhausted my units.

2. Spending. I only considered buildings on turn 4, after the numbers showed Funds were my limit and manpower was piling up (10k to 53k). I had seen the Buildings tab only after turn 1 because the first turn felt like it was all about orders and the briefing never pointed at it. I was hesitant because upkeep and spending everything on units seemed safer. Funds limited me early (9 or 15 per turn bought one unit). Manpower was always in excess: I never used more than about a quarter of it, and the 53k stockpile felt wasted. From turn 5 funds also piled up (I left 15, then about 16 unspent) because I had no obvious thing to buy; recruit order slots (2, then 3 with the Factory) became the limit, not money.

3. Never used: air units and the Leon recon flight, trucks (+1 move, I never saw the value), Rail yard, Airfield, Hospital, Fortifications, Farms, Disband, Replenish (only seen), Sack (Mola's ability; unclear and sounded risky), Withdraw, Logistics and Economy map layers, foreign or political options (none visible). I ignored them because there was no pressing need or I lacked clear information on their value. Interesting: where to put the Mola stack, whether to entrench or attack, what to build. Forced: the default attack order in contested ground (it picks for you and wears you out), and recruiting veterans every turn because they were the best deal.

4. Attack or hold. I used the destination odds preview. It showed ratios, dug-in levels, barricades, mountain entry and a percent to take the territory. It told me Madrid (1:6, 0%) was impossible and that Cantabria at 3:1 was still only 10% because the defender was dug in level 3 and held a city. So digging in is very strong; I trusted it for my own stacks (Navarra at level 3 was 24-29). I attacked only where I had a big numeric edge and a pending relief need (Asturias) or the weakest enemy (Cantabria). I never found out whether a 10% shot was worth the fatigue and losses; I attacked anyway.

5. Missing information. Hard to find: exact current counts of my territories and cities (I only looked at the briefing on turn 1), how close each other side was to the thresholds (I never saw the other factions' progress; I guessed from the map colours, thinking the South was nearest with Andalucia, Castilla-La Mancha and Murcia and the Republic collapsing), what Funds income was composed of (I saw only +N next turn), what the other factions were building, and whether a move command had committed (several UI uncertainties). Also unclear: what "to take it" percent meant and what Sack and some buildings do beyond one line.

6. Three changes. (a) Make the economy visible on turn 1: a prompt or briefing line that Factory/Mines raise income and that units are not the only use of Funds, plus show an income breakdown. (b) Fix the interface: units' counters blocking territory selection, a clear confirmation for moves, and a warning when the default attack order will exhaust units. (c) Show victory progress for all three sides, not just mine, and give manpower a useful sink (or cap its accrual) so a big stockpile is not dead weight; also make fortified defenders less all-powerful or give attackers a way (artillery, air) that is explained in the odds preview.
