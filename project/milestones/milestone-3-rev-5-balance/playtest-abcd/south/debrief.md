# South debrief

1. Winning. Briefing said: reach 10 of 18 territories or 19 of 25 cities. Turn 1 I just brought all of Africa across to take Andalucia (worked: 42 attack vs 6). Turns 2-3 I pushed into Murcia and Castilla-La Mancha, then hit a wall: after turn 3 every Republican city was dug in to level 3 and the attack odds were 0-20%. I switched to "build a bigger stack, replenish, add artillery" and made a gamble at Valencia on turn 6 (result unknown to me when I stopped). Plan changed from fast march to siege. Starting again: I would not take Murcia on turn 2 with a thin split; I would keep the whole army together and strike Valencia/Madrid on turns 2-3 before digging in reached level 3, and I'd recruit artillery on turn 1-2 instead of turn 4. Also consider that I wasted two turns (4-5) waiting. I am not sure that striking earlier would have worked: Murcia cost 5,109 men even at 5:1.

2. Buildings and economy. I saw the Building tab, Production tab and the AI's farms/mines/factories in the turn reports, but I never spent on them. Reason: Funds were always spent on units (veterans 9, artillery 8) and I never had a clear picture of what a building would pay back or how fast; the war pressure felt more urgent. That may have been a mistake given I only earned about 22 Funds a turn. Limiting resource: Funds. Manpower I had far more than I could use (53k and +12k a turn, using about 3k per turn). Funds ran to single digits after each recruiting round; Manpower piled up unused. So I had surplus manpower and a funds bottleneck, which makes the manpower numbers feel irrelevant.

3. Never used: bombers (grounded by rain, and I never found a mission order), tanks, trucks (+1 move), Sack with a general, disband, buildings/production, fighters, recon, amphibious beyond turn 1, reinforcing by sea later, replenish only used twice. Interesting: replenish (very cheap), the split-or-combine attack choice, rail-then-attack, artillery. Forced: turn-1 crossing (obvious), recruiting vets every turn (the best Funds-to-attack ratio, no real alternatives), holding while dug in.

4. Attack or hold. I used the destination odds preview as a go/no-go. It reads "approx X:1, N percent" plus the modifiers (dug in +6, barricades, generals). It told me clearly that dug in level 3 made Valencia 0-20% even at 54 vs 16, and that being passive costs me since they dig further. What it did not tell me: it only scores the selected group, ignoring artillery's roll bonus and other groups joining, so combined odds were a guess. Digging in also protected my own stacks, but dig-in level 3 for everyone made the board stagnate by turn 4.

5. Missing information. I could not see: how far each side was from victory (no territory/city count for the others; only my own meter), how many turns the game lasts, what a building actually yields, the enemy's recruiting queue, and where the Republic's reserves were. I kept guessing about Northern's progress: they took Aragon and Pais Vasco and massed units, but I never saw a number. Hard to find: details of battles are inside a long Last turn pane; the actual territory counts per side. I thought I was far from winning (about 3 of 18); Northern looked somewhat closer to Madrid than me; the Republic looked weak in the field but strongly fortified. A plan-changing fact would be a clear rival score and a turn limit.

6. Three changes.
a) Show a combined-odds preview that includes everything already ordered against the target (other groups, artillery bonus, generals) so players can see the real chance before committing, and make destination commits undoable (clicking a destination commits at once).
b) Make the middle game less static: dig-in grows to level 3 within three turns for everyone, so attacks on cities become hopeless. Slow it, or make breaking dig-in easier (artillery cheaper/earlier, or siege that wears it down), or give a clear shortcut such as air support that is not weather-locked.
c) Show every side's victory progress and the turn limit, and give a plain summary of what buildings/developments pay back, so economy spending is a real option rather than something I ignored. Also tidy the Funds vs Manpower imbalance: manpower was abundant while Funds were tight.
