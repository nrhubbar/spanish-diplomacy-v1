# Republic debrief

1. Winning. Briefing said 10 of 18 territories or 19 of 25 cities; I started at 6 and 13. On turn 1 I treated it as "hold Madrid, recruit a bit, see what comes". By turn 2 (Aragon and Andalucia lost, Murcia soon after) the plan became survival: dig in everywhere, avoid battles, hope attackers bleed. By turn 6 I had no real winning plan, only not losing. Different turn 1: I would not recruit a militia column (3k manpower plus 1.5k upkeep each turn, and I later had to disband it). I would not leave units in contested ground on "attack" by default (Aragon, Andalucia, Murcia garrison units were lost or thrown away). I would withdraw or concentrate them in Madrid/Valencia/Cataluna early, keep manpower reserve, and spend Funds on farms and fortifications on turn 1 while I still had 10k manpower. I did not check the manpower ledger until turn 3; I should have done it on turn 1.
2. Spending. Buildings came to mind on turn 2 once I found the Buildings tab: fortifications (+2 defence), mines (+funds), farms (+0.6k manpower) were cheap in Funds, so I bought them. Factory (4k manpower) and fortifications (2k) became impossible once manpower hit 0 on turn 3. Manpower was the binding limit nearly all game; Funds piled up unused (40 to 61 idle). So Funds were abundant and manpower scarce; upkeep, especially militia at 1.5k, ate the income. Farms were my only lever and builds seemed slow (a Farms 1->2 queued on turn 2 still had 2 turns left on turn 6; I am unsure why, maybe it was waiting on something).
3. Never used: air missions, recruiting aircraft, replenish (greyed: upkeep left too little manpower), production tab, rail yard/rail moves, tanks and artillery, Sack (Miaja's ability, seen but not understood), Withdraw except once, deliberate Move orders (I never moved a unit voluntarily). Reasons: no manpower, no clear payoff, and fear of losing dug-in works. Interesting: the disband decision on turn 3 (ledger said exactly which units cost what) and whether to attack Asturias on turn 4. Forced: nearly everything else; with 0 manpower there was nothing to decide and the game became "press End Turn".
4. Attack vs hold. The briefing example (1:1 odds = 10% to take, 2:1 = 25%) made attacking look poor unless I had a big ratio, and my units have attack 1-4 while enemy stacks have 12-54. Dug-in level 3 gives +6 to the defender, which in my reading made holding far better than attacking; any move forfeits the works. I never saw an odds preview for a planned attack because I never set one up, so I cannot say how good it is. The enemy also did not attack my dug-in stacks for turns 4-6, which suggests they had the same read.
5. What would change my plan: seeing the enemies' manpower and upkeep, the odds preview before committing, and knowing how long builds take and what stalls them. Hard to find: the manpower ledger (I found it only when a warning showed up), what "contested" costs (no income, builds paused), and what a standing battle's default does. Closeness: enemies hold roughly 11-12 of 18 territories by my guess and are massing 54-strength and 27-strength stacks; I think they are nearer to a win than I am, but unsure since I never saw their counts. I was at about 5 territories, so far from 10.
6. Three changes. (a) Show manpower upkeep and a projected ledger on turn 1 and warn before recruiting that it cuts income (militia upkeep 1.5k is a trap). (b) Give Funds a use when manpower is short (convert Funds to manpower, buy equipment, hire mercenaries, or let buildings that cost only Funds do more), because idle Funds felt like a design gap. (c) Make contested-ground defaults clearer: units start on "attack" in battles I did not choose, and a short pre-turn-1 note listing which units are in standing battles and what withdraw does would prevent early losses.
